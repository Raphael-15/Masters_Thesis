## Project context

The thesis examines the techno-economic performance of co-located photovoltaic (PV) and battery energy storage systems (BESS) within energy communities. It situates the research in the context of renewable integration, distributional effects, and community-scale flexibility.

## Research questions and objectives

The core research questions focus on: (1) the technical impacts of co-located PV and storage on key operational KPIs (self-consumption, self-sufficiency, reduction in peak import), (2) the techno-economic implications under different tariff and incentive regimes, and (3) policy/recommendation insights for energy communities.

## Approach overview

The Introduction outlines a data-driven methodology: combine measured or synthetic high-resolution community load profiles with PV generation scenarios; build a battery operational model capturing real-world constraints; and compute economic KPIs to assess viability.

## Expected contributions

The research promises three contributions: a reproducible, data-centric modelling pipeline for assessing PV+BESS at community scale; a set of scenario results that identify technical and economic trade-offs; and practical recommendations for community stakeholders.
